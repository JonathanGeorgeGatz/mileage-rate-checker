THE MILEAGE RATE CHECKER — the three prompts, word for word, in the order they were sent
(Build It With AI, Episode 30. The answers to prompts 1 and 2 are in the bundle as
mileage_v1_FIRST_BUILD_NOT_THE_TOOL.gs and mileage_v2_SECOND_BUILD_NOT_THE_TOOL.gs.)

---------------------------------------------------------------- PROMPT 1
We run a small home care agency and pay our aides back for driving from a Google Sheet. One tab,
Trips, has Date driven, Employee, From, To, Odometer start, Odometer end, Miles, Purpose, Date
submitted, Rate paid and Amount paid for every trip. We pay the IRS rate, 72.5 cents a mile.
Write me an Apps Script with a menu that works out what each person should have been paid and
tells me who we overpaid or underpaid.

---------------------------------------------------------------- PROMPT 2
That rate is out of date. The IRS raised it to 76 cents a mile on July 13, 2026, for miles driven
on or after July 1, 2026. Before July 1 it stays 72.5 cents. Rebuild it to follow this exactly:
- Put the rates in a Settings tab: From, To, Rate per mile. The rate for a trip is the one for the
  DATE IT WAS DRIVEN. Never the date it was submitted, never the date it was paid.
- If a trip has both odometer readings, the miles are end minus start. If the typed Miles don't
  match the odometer, refuse the row and say both numbers. Never guess a distance.
- Refuse any row with no employee, no business purpose or a date you cannot read, with its row
  number and the reason. A date no rate covers is set aside, not priced.
- The same person, date, route and odometer twice is one trip. Keep the first, refuse the copy.
- Work in cents. Round each trip to the cent, half a cent up.
Then I'll check every number against a separate checker written from these rules.

---------------------------------------------------------------- PROMPT 3
The checker disagreed with you on 38 trips. Fix the three causes: "on or after July 1" means a
trip driven ON July 1 gets 76 cents; the odometer wins over typed miles, and a mismatch is refused,
not used; and half a cent rounds up, it is not dropped. Then make the sheet check itself before
I trust it:
- Find every column by its header, in any order. If a tab's header row is missing or wrong,
  price nothing from it and say which tab.
- If one person's odometer readings overlap, the same miles are being claimed twice: refuse the
  later trip and name the row it overlaps.
- Count in public: rows in = checked + set aside + refused, every time.
- Add up the money twice, by trip and by person, and compare to the cent.
- If any of that fails, or more than 10% of the rows are refused, write
  DO NOT TRUST THESE MILEAGE TOTALS across the top of the report, in red, and leave it there.
- List every trip paid the wrong amount, and say why: the rate it was paid at and the rate for
  the day it was driven.
